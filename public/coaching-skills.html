From Telling to Asking: Coaching Skills for Leaders

Help people find their own answers. Listen closely, ask questions that open up new thinking, observe without bias, and turn coaching into a structured practice.

Two-day live course Limited to 16 participants Interactive workshops Online or on-site

Challenge

Giving the answer is fast. Helping people find it lasts.

Most leaders got where they are by solving problems. So when someone brings them a problem, they solve it. It feels efficient, but people stop thinking for themselves, the same questions keep coming back, and the leader becomes the bottleneck. Coaching offers another way, and it is a skill that can be learned.

Advice given before the problem is understood
Conversations that drift to your own experience
Questions that already contain the answer
Judgements formed after a single meeting
People who keep coming back with the same problem
Good intentions to coach, but no structure to follow

Outcomes

What you'll leave with

Know when you're coaching and when you're not

Move deliberately between coaching, teaching, mentoring, advising and role-modelling, and find your way back to coaching.

Listen at a deeper level

Use the three levels of listening and support responses so that people feel heard and keep thinking.

Ask questions that open up new thinking

Create powerful questions with Karl Tomm's model, from understanding the past to agreeing next steps.

Observe without jumping to conclusions

Separate facts from interpretations, recognise common biases and test your hypotheses before acting on them.

Coach with a clear structure

Move from observations and hypotheses to behavioural goals, metrics and the right coaching tools.

Hold a complete coaching conversation

Agree a purpose, explore before converging, and practise with peers in a coaching dojo.

Curriculum

Curriculum

What coaching is and how it compares with systemic and sports coaching, and the five stances a coach moves between: coaching, teaching, mentoring, advising and role-modelling.

Curiosity and keywords, communication enablers, the three levels of listening, shift and support responses, and powerful questions using Karl Tomm's model.

The Gemba Walk, what to observe, reinforcing and deepening observations, the story of Clever Hans, cognitive biases, and forming and testing hypotheses.

A structured coaching approach with observations, hypotheses, behavioural goals, metrics and coaching tools, and complete coaching conversations practised in a coaching dojo. The course closes with a personal action plan.
