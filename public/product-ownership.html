Challenge

Delivering more features does not mean creating more value.

Many people take on product ownership after working in projects, IT or delivery roles, where success means shipping the agreed scope on time. Product work is measured differently: by the change it creates for users and for the business. Without that shift, teams stay busy while the product does not move forward.

Features built before the problem is understood
Backlogs that grow with every request
Priorities set by the loudest or most senior voice
Little direct contact with the people who use the product
Roadmaps that promise dates rather than outcomes
Success measured by output rather than outcome

Outcomes

What you'll leave with

Connect your work to a clear product vision

Draft a concise product vision and link it to measurable goals that guide everyday decisions.

Understand the people you build for

Use personas, empathy maps and customer interviews to replace assumptions with evidence.

Choose the problems worth solving

Explore opportunities together with stakeholders before committing to a solution.

Write user stories that deliver value

Write clear acceptance criteria and split large stories into small, testable pieces of work.

Prioritise and plan for outcomes

Use story mapping, Cost of Delay, WSJF and the Kano model, and build a Now-Next-Later roadmap based on outcomes rather than dates.

Keep stakeholders informed and involved

Map your stakeholders, decide how much attention each one needs, and manage expectations openly.

Curriculum

Curriculum

Move from output to outcome, create a product vision, understand users through personas, empathy maps and interviews, explore opportunities with the Opportunity Canvas, and turn them into user stories with clear acceptance criteria.

Organise work with story mapping, prioritise with Cost of Delay, WSJF and the Kano model, build an outcome-based roadmap, and engage stakeholders deliberately. The course closes with common pitfalls and a personal action plan.
