Challenge

Decisions slow down when ownership is unclear, too many people need to agree, or disagreement is mistaken for misalignment.

Good organisational decision-making is not about getting everyone to agree. It requires clarity about the decision, who should contribute, who decides, and what alignment means once the decision is made.

Decisions involve more people than necessary
It is unclear who actually owns the decision
Discussion continues after enough information is available
Disagreement is treated as a failure to align
Decisions are reopened without new information
People leave with different interpretations of what was decided

Outcomes

What you'll leave with

Frame decisions clearly

Separate the decision itself from the surrounding discussion and clarify what needs to be resolved.

Choose who should participate and how

Distinguish decision ownership from input, consultation, and implementation.

Navigate disagreement productively

Surface competing perspectives and use disagreement to improve decisions without requiring consensus.

Create alignment and closure

Make decisions explicit, communicate the rationale, and reduce unnecessary reopening afterwards.

Curriculum

Curriculum

Learn to frame a decision clearly, choose who should be involved and in which role, and select a decision method that fits the situation. Apply the tools to a decision that is currently stuck in your own work.

Practise using disagreement to improve a decision, moving a group from discussion to a clear outcome, and communicating the decision and its rationale. Leave with a decision protocol you can use in your own team.
